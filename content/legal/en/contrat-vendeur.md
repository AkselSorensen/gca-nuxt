### GSA — SELLER CONTRACT

### Section 1 — Purpose, role of the parties and how the partnership works

### Article 1 — Purpose of the contract

This contract defines the conditions under which GSA provides the seller with services related to the presentation, connection with buyers and promotion of digital content offered by the seller. These services may notably include: - integration of the content into the GSA catalog; - prior verification of the content; - creation and management of its presentation on the platform; - creation of presentation and promotion materials; - promotion of the content to users; - connection with buyers; - technical management of orders and payments; - support and first-level assistance after acquisition. This contract does not constitute an employment contract, a general agency mandate or a partnership relationship between GSA and the seller.

### Article 2 — Independence of the parties

The seller carries out their activity independently. This contract creates between GSA and the seller: - no subordination; - no employment relationship; - no joint company; - no association; - no general mandate allowing one party to bind the other. Each party remains responsible for its own legal, administrative, tax and social obligations. The seller notably remains responsible for their professional status and the obligations incumbent upon them in respect of their activity.

### Article 3 — GSA's role

GSA provides a service notably enabling sellers and users interested in their content to meet. In this context, GSA may ensure: - the reception of content provided by the seller; - its prior verification; - its preparation for presentation; - its publication on the platform; - its promotion; - the management of its commercial presentation; - the technical management of orders; - user support; - the transmission of requests requiring the seller's intervention. GSA remains free to determine the technical and editorial arrangements used to present content, subject to the commitments expressly provided in this contract.

### Article 4 — No direct publication by the seller

The seller does not publish their content directly on the GSA platform. Any content intended to be offered on GSA must be provided to GSA in accordance with the arrangements provided by the platform or communicated to the seller. GSA then verifies the content before any publication. GSA may notably: - check the files; - check the coherence of the information provided; - check the presence of the announced elements; - request corrections or additional information; - refuse the publication of content; - request a modification of its presentation. The publication of content therefore falls under GSA's validation process.

### Article 5 — Selection and acceptance of content

GSA reserves the right to accept or refuse any content offered by a seller. This decision may notably be based on: - the quality of the content; - its compliance with GSA's rules; - its compatibility with the services offered; - the information provided by the seller; - the rights attached to the content; - technical considerations; - security considerations; - the coherence of the content with GSA's offering. The seller has no automatic right to the publication of content provided to GSA.

### Article 6 — Human verification

Content offered by sellers is subject, within the limits of reasonably available means, to human verification prior to publication. This verification notably aims to identify obvious anomalies concerning: - the files; - the presentation; - the coherence of the content; - the announced elements; - any apparent technical or security risks. However, this verification does not constitute an exhaustive certification of the content. GSA notably cannot guarantee that human verification will detect: - all technical errors; - all possible vulnerabilities; - any violation of third-party rights; - any malicious element hidden in a file; - any incompatibility with a particular technical environment. The seller remains responsible for their content and the rights necessary for its exploitation.

### Article 7 — Promotion of content

GSA may ensure the presentation and promotion of selected content. In this context, GSA may notably create or modify: - thumbnails; - screenshots; - presentation videos; - descriptions; - presentation texts; - graphic elements; - promotional materials. These elements may be created by GSA to improve the presentation and visibility of the content on the platform. The seller authorizes GSA to make the adaptations necessary for this presentation.

### Article 8 — Authorization to use the content by GSA

To enable the performance of the services provided in this contract, the seller grants GSA, for the duration of the partnership and for the needs of its services, an authorization to use the content provided. This authorization notably allows GSA to: - store the files necessary for the performance of its services; - examine the content; - present the content on its platform; - create presentation materials; - take screenshots or demonstrations; - promote the content; - present the seller and their achievements within GSA services. This authorization does not constitute a transfer of intellectual property to GSA. The seller remains the holder of the rights they hold over their content.

### Article 9 — No exclusivity

Unless otherwise agreed in writing, this contract does not impose any exclusivity on the seller. The seller remains free to offer their creations on other platforms or by their own means. This freedom does not however call into question the rights granted to GSA for the performance of this contract or the obligations relating to content already offered on the platform.

### Article 10 — General commitment of the seller

The seller acknowledges that the quality and legality of the content offered are essential elements of the partnership. They notably undertake to: - provide content over which they hold the necessary rights; - provide accurate information; - report any particular restriction applicable to content; - comply with third-party licenses used in their creations; - not deliberately provide malicious content; - inform GSA of any difficulty affecting already published content; - cooperate reasonably with GSA when intervention is required.

### Article 11 — No guarantee of sales volume

GSA does not guarantee any minimum volume of: - visitors; - downloads; - orders; - turnover; - revenue paid to the seller. GSA's services aim to facilitate the presentation and connection between sellers and users, without guaranteeing a determined commercial result.

### Article 12 — Evolution of the partnership

GSA may evolve the technical arrangements for presenting, referencing or promoting content to evolve its services. Where these changes substantially modify the seller's obligations or the financial terms agreed with them, the applicable provisions will be communicated to the seller under the conditions provided in this contract.

### Section 2 — Provision of content, compliance and seller obligations

### Article 13 — Provision of content to GSA

The seller provides GSA with the content they wish to offer on the platform in accordance with the technical arrangements communicated by GSA. The elements provided may notably include: - the files necessary for the use of the content; - the additional files necessary for its operation; - technical information; - prerequisites; - installation or usage instructions; - license information; - the information necessary for the presentation of the content. The seller ensures that the files provided are usable and correspond to the content presented to GSA.

### Article 14 — Accuracy of the information provided

The seller warrants the accuracy, truthfulness and currency of the information communicated to GSA about their content. They notably must report: - known technical limitations; - required software or versions; - any dependencies; - applicable third-party licenses; - use restrictions; - elements that cannot be redistributed; - any other element likely to affect the normal use of the content. The seller undertakes to inform GSA as soon as possible of any substantial change affecting already published content.

### Article 15 — Ownership and rights over the content

The seller warrants holding the rights necessary to provide the content to GSA and allow its exploitation within the services offered. They notably warrant that the content provided: - is their creation when presented as such; - or is used in accordance with the rights and licenses they hold; - may be presented and distributed within GSA's operations; - does not, to their knowledge, infringe a third party's intellectual property rights. The seller remains responsible for verifying the rights attached to the elements they provide.

### Article 16 — Content originating from third parties

When content includes elements created by third parties, the seller must ensure that the rights they hold allow their use within this contract. The seller must notably verify that the licenses concerned authorize, where necessary: - the integration of the elements into their content; - their provision to users; - their presentation on GSA; - their use in the necessary presentation materials; - the planned modifications or adaptations. The seller undertakes to keep the evidence enabling their rights to be established where such proof may reasonably be necessary.

### Article 17 — Prohibited content

The seller must not provide GSA with content that is: - illegal; - fraudulent; - malicious; - intended to compromise a computer system; - deliberately containing malware; - manifestly infringing a third party's rights; - containing personal data obtained or used unlawfully; - or contrary to the rules applicable to GSA services. GSA may refuse or remove any content whose presence on the platform presents an obvious risk or contravenes the applicable rules.

### Article 18 — File security

The seller undertakes to take reasonable precautions to ensure that the files provided to GSA do not deliberately contain malicious programs, hidden mechanisms or features intended to compromise users' security. When GSA identifies or suspects a security problem, it may suspend the publication or access to the content concerned while carrying out the necessary verifications. In case of serious risk, GSA may immediately remove the content concerned.

### Article 19 — Compatibility and functioning

The seller provides GSA with the information necessary to identify the normal conditions of use of the content. They must notably indicate, where relevant: - the platform concerned; - the compatible versions; - the necessary dependencies; - the required software; - known limitations; - specific installation conditions. The seller may not present as compatible a technical environment which they know or should reasonably know does not allow the normal functioning of the content.

### Article 20 — Updating and correcting content

When a major defect affecting content is identified after its publication, the seller undertakes to cooperate reasonably with GSA to seek its resolution. Depending on the situation, this cooperation may notably include: - providing a corrected version; - transmitting technical information; - correcting a defective file; - responding to technical requests transmitted by GSA. The seller is not required to develop new features for free that were not part of the content initially offered.

### Article 21 — Substantial modification of content

Any substantial modification of already published content likely to affect: - its functioning; - its compatibility; - its content; - its conditions of use; - its dependencies; - or the usage rights granted to buyers, must be reported to GSA within a reasonable period. GSA may request a new verification before making a new version available.

### Article 22 — Duty of cooperation

The seller undertakes to cooperate with GSA when intervention is required concerning one of their content. This cooperation may notably be requested in case of: - a technical problem; - a user report; - a complaint; - a suspected rights violation; - a security problem; - a request for information concerning a license. The seller undertakes to provide a response within a reasonable period given the nature of the problem.

### Article 23 — Claims relating to third-party rights

When a third party claims to hold rights over content offered by the seller, GSA may request any element enabling the situation to be assessed. The seller undertakes to cooperate in the examination of the claim. Where the situation justifies it, GSA may temporarily suspend the publication or access to the content concerned pending resolution of the dispute. This suspension may be made on a precautionary basis and does not, by itself, constitute an acknowledgement of the merits of the claim.

### Article 24 — Seller's liability

The seller remains responsible for the content provided to GSA and the rights they claim to hold over it. They notably assume the consequences resulting from: - a lack of sufficient rights; - deliberately erroneous information; - a violation of a third-party license; - the provision of malicious content; - the concealment of an important restriction; - a known defect that was not reported. This liability applies within the limits provided by law and by the other provisions of this contract.

### Article 25 — Preventive removal of content

GSA may temporarily suspend or remove content when it reasonably considers that its presence on the platform notably presents: - a legal risk; - a security risk; - a significant risk to users; - a violation of GSA's rules; - a potential infringement of a third party's rights. Where possible, GSA informs the seller of the reason for the removal and allows them to provide the information necessary to resolve the situation.

### Article 26 — No guarantee of publication

Providing content to GSA does not guarantee its publication. GSA may refuse content when it does not meet the platform's criteria or when its publication is not compatible with the services offered. The seller cannot claim compensation merely because of the refusal or postponement of the publication of content. Section 3 — Publication, presentation and promotion of content

### Article 27 — Preparation of content before publication

After receiving content, GSA may carry out the operations necessary for its preparation before publication. These operations may notably include: - checking the structure of the files; - checking the elements necessary for the use of the content; - organizing the files; - preparing the product page; - adapting the information provided by the seller; - creating or modifying the visual elements necessary for its presentation. These operations are carried out within GSA's services and do not constitute a modification of the intellectual property of the content.

### Article 28 — Creation of the content page

GSA ensures the creation and management of the presentation of the content it decides to publish. The page may notably include: - the name of the content; - its description; - its characteristics; - its prerequisites; - screenshots; - images; - a presentation video; - license information; - any information useful to the user. GSA may organize and present this information in the format and according to the standards it applies to its platform.

### Article 29 — Adaptation of the information provided by the seller

GSA may correct obvious errors, reformulate or adapt the information provided by the seller to ensure a presentation consistent with the platform's standards. These adaptations must remain faithful to the essential characteristics of the content. GSA may not deliberately present content as having features or characteristics that it knows it does not have. The seller remains responsible for the accuracy of the technical and functional information communicated to GSA.

### Article 30 — Creation of visual materials by GSA

Within its services, GSA may create elements intended to present and promote the seller's content. These elements may notably include: - thumbnails; - screenshots; - graphic compositions; - promotional images; - videos; - animations; - presentation texts; - graphic elements associated with the content page. The seller authorizes GSA to use the content provided for this purpose.

### Article 31 — GSA's rights over its own creations

The original elements created by GSA to present or promote content remain the property of GSA, subject to any rights held by third parties over the elements incorporated into these creations. This notably concerns the graphic elements, compositions, texts, video edits and other original creations made by GSA. The seller acquires no property right over these elements merely because they represent or present their content.

### Article 32 — Use of promotional materials by the seller

Unless GSA authorizes otherwise, the seller may not reuse for their own account the original materials created by GSA specifically for the presentation of their content. It is notably forbidden to directly reuse: - a thumbnail created by GSA; - a video produced by GSA; - a graphic composition; - an original promotional text; - an edit made by GSA; to promote the content on another platform or within an independent commercial activity. This restriction does not concern elements over which the seller themselves held rights before their use by GSA.

### Article 33 — Promotion of content

The seller authorizes GSA to promote published content within its services. This promotion may notably be carried out: - on the GSA platform; - on social networks operated by GSA; - in commercial communications related to GSA services; - in presentation videos; - in publications or campaigns intended to present the content available on GSA. This authorization is granted solely to allow GSA to promote its services and the content offered on its platform.

### Article 34 — Use of the seller's name and pseudonym

To identify the creator of content, GSA may use the trade name, pseudonym or username communicated by the seller. This use may notably appear: - on the content page; - in communications relating to the content; - in rankings or selections; - in GSA presentation materials. The seller warrants holding the rights necessary to allow this use when they provide a name, brand or identification element to GSA.

### Article 35 — Use of content in GSA's portfolio

GSA may present the content published on its platform as part of the presentation of its activity and services. This presentation may notably take the form of: - a portfolio; - a page presenting the achievements available on GSA; - a summary video; - a presentation of the platform; - statistics or content selections. This use remains limited to the presentation of GSA's services and does not constitute an appropriation of the seller's content.

### Article 36 — Authorization granted to GSA

To enable the performance of this contract, the seller grants GSA a license to use the content provided, limited to the following needs: - verification; - technical storage; - publication; - presentation; - promotion; - provision to buyers in accordance with the applicable conditions; - support management; - retention of the elements necessary for order tracking. This authorization is: - non-exclusive; - limited to the needs of GSA services; - valid for the period necessary for the performance of the contract and compliance with the obligations arising from it. It does not entail any transfer of intellectual property to GSA.

### Article 37 — Territorial scope

The authorization granted to GSA covers the territories in which its services may be accessible. This provision is notably necessary to allow GSA to present and promote content to users located in different countries. It does not however confer any additional right on GSA over the content outside the uses provided in this contract.

### Article 38 — Maintaining the presentation after removal

When content is removed from the platform, GSA may temporarily retain certain elements necessary for: - the follow-up of past orders; - the management of already granted licenses; - the retention of contractual documents; - compliance with its legal obligations; - the processing of complaints or disputes. Unless a particular need exists, GSA stops actively promoting removed content.

### Article 39 — Removal requested by the seller

The seller may request the removal of content from the platform under the conditions provided in this contract. The removal ends its availability for new orders, subject to: - orders already completed; - licenses already granted to buyers; - GSA's legal or contractual obligations; - the needs related to the processing of complaints. The removal of content therefore does not necessarily constitute a revocation of the rights already granted to buyers before its removal.

### Article 40 — Limits of the authorization granted to GSA

GSA may not use the seller's content outside the purposes provided in this contract without obtaining additional authorization where necessary. This contract notably does not allow GSA to: - claim ownership of the content; - sell the content in its own name outside the operation provided by the platform; - grant a third party rights exceeding those provided by the license applicable to the content; - deliberately present the content as GSA's own creation.

### Section 4 — Price, GSA remuneration and payout of amounts

### Article 41 — Determination of the sale price

The price applicable to each content is determined within the commercial terms agreed between GSA and the seller. The applicable price is communicated to the seller before the publication of the content concerned. Unless otherwise agreed, the price displayed on the platform constitutes the amount invoiced to the buyer for the acquisition of the content and the associated license.

### Article 42 — GSA remuneration

In consideration for the services provided to the seller, GSA receives remuneration on the transactions carried out through its services. The calculation methods of this remuneration are determined individually with each seller. They may notably be defined in: - this contract; - the commercial terms agreed between the parties; - a supplementary document or agreement accepted by the seller. No particular remuneration rate is presumed applicable in the absence of an agreement between the parties.

### Article 43 — Payment processing

Payments made by buyers are processed by the payment provider used by GSA, notably Stripe. GSA does not necessarily physically receive all the amounts paid by the buyer before their distribution. The payment provider ensures the technical and financial processing of the transaction in accordance with its own terms.

### Article 44 — Distribution of amounts from a transaction

When a transaction is carried out, the amount corresponding to the order is processed by the payment provider. After taking into account the fees applicable to payment processing, the amounts are distributed in accordance with the terms agreed between GSA and the seller. The distribution notably includes: - the fees charged by the payment provider; - the remuneration due to GSA for its services; - the amount due to the seller for the transaction. The seller acknowledges that the payment provider's fees may be deducted before determining the amount due to them, depending on how the payment system used works.

### Article 45 — Role of the payment provider

Stripe or any other payment provider used by GSA may notably ensure: - the collection of payment; - the verification and processing of the transaction; - the deduction of applicable fees; - the distribution of amounts; - the payment of the amounts due to the parties concerned. Payment operations are also subject to the contractual terms and rules of the payment provider concerned.

### Article 46 — Conditions necessary for the seller's payment

The seller must provide the information necessary for the opening and maintenance of their payment account with the provider used by GSA. They notably must provide information that is: - accurate; - complete; - up to date. The seller is responsible for complying with the identity verification, compliance procedures and obligations imposed on them by the payment provider.

### Article 47 — Blocking or suspension of a payment

A payment intended for the seller may be delayed, suspended or blocked when the payment provider or GSA is legally or technically required to do so. This may notably occur in case of: - identity verification; - compliance control; - suspected fraud; - payment dispute; - refund request; - chargeback; - incomplete or incorrect information; - a restriction imposed by the payment provider. GSA cannot be held responsible for a delay directly attributable to the payment provider or to an applicable regulatory procedure.

### Article 48 — Cancellation, refund and chargeback

When a transaction is cancelled, refunded or subject to a payment dispute, the corresponding amounts may be deducted from the amounts due to the seller in accordance with the applicable rules and the payment provider's terms. When the origin of the problem is attributable to the content provided by the seller or to a failure by the seller to comply with their contractual obligations, the corresponding amounts may notably be charged to the seller under the conditions provided in this contract and within the limits authorized by law.

### Article 49 — Payment fees

The fees charged by the payment provider are independent of GSA's remuneration. They are applied in accordance with the provider's pricing terms. GSA may use another payment provider in the future, provided that the applicable operating arrangements are communicated where necessary.

### Article 50 — No guaranteed remuneration

The conclusion of this contract does not guarantee the seller any minimum amount of income. The seller's remuneration notably depends on: - the number of transactions carried out; - the price of the content; - the commercial terms agreed with GSA; - any refunds or disputes; - the fees applicable to transactions.

### Article 51 — Tax and social obligations of the seller

The seller remains responsible for their own tax, accounting, social and administrative obligations related to the amounts they receive within their activity. GSA does not provide personalized tax or accounting advice to the seller. The seller must notably ensure that their status allows them to carry out their activity and receive the corresponding income.

### Article 52 — Transaction traceability

Transaction information may be retained by GSA and/or the payment provider notably to enable: - order tracking; - the calculation of amounts due; - refund management; - dispute resolution; - compliance with legal and accounting obligations. The data is retained in accordance with the applicable data protection and document retention obligations.

### Section 5 — Support, incidents and seller obligations after acquisition

### Article 53 — Support provided by GSA

Within its services, GSA provides a first-level support service intended for buyers who have acquired content through the platform. This support notably aims to: - respond to requests relating to access to content; - check any missing files; - guide the user in the general use of the content; - identify the apparent origin of a problem; - forward to the seller the requests requiring their intervention. GSA may also follow up the request until its resolution or until the transmission of the necessary information to the seller.

### Article 54 — Support period after acquisition

Unless otherwise indicated, GSA provides the initial follow-up of requests relating to content for a period of seven (7) days from the purchase. This period corresponds to the support and assistance period provided by GSA within its services. It does not limit: - the applicable legal warranties; - the seller's contractual obligations; - the rights enjoyed by the buyer under the regulations; - the interventions necessary in case of a major problem.

### Article 55 — First level of incident handling

When a buyer reports a problem, GSA may carry out an initial analysis to determine whether it notably concerns: - access to the content; - a missing file; - an obvious error in the delivery; - the information presented on the platform; - a technical problem requiring the seller's intervention. When GSA can directly resolve the problem, it may do so within its support service. When the seller's intervention is necessary, GSA forwards the relevant information to them.

### Article 56 — Seller intervention

The seller undertakes to cooperate reasonably with GSA when an assistance request directly concerns their content. They may notably be invited to: - analyze a technical problem; - provide a correction; - provide a missing file; - provide a new version of the content; - explain an installation procedure; - answer a technical question; - provide any information necessary to resolve the problem. The seller remains responsible for problems directly relating to the content they provided.

### Article 57 — Seller response time

When a request is forwarded to them by GSA, the seller undertakes to provide a response within a reasonable period given the nature and urgency of the problem. GSA may define specific support arrangements in the terms applicable to the seller. The repeated absence of a response to legitimate requests may constitute a breach of the seller's obligations and lead to the measures provided in this contract.

### Article 58 — Major problems

A major problem notably means a problem making the content substantially unusable under the announced conditions or presenting a significant risk to the user. This may notably concern: - unusable content; - missing essential files; - incompatibility with the announced environment; - a major technical defect; - the presence of a malicious element; - a serious security problem; - content manifestly different from what was presented. When such a problem is identified, GSA may ask the seller to intervene as soon as possible.

### Article 59 — Malicious content or security risk

When content is suspected of containing a malicious element or presenting a serious risk to users, GSA may immediately: - suspend its availability; - prevent any new acquisition; - suspend its promotion; - carry out the necessary verifications; - request explanations from the seller. Where necessary, GSA may also inform the users concerned and take measures to limit the risk. The seller must fully cooperate in the analysis and resolution of the incident.

### Article 60 — Corrections and updates

When a defect substantially affecting content is confirmed, the seller may be invited to provide a correction or a new version. The correction must, where reasonably possible: - resolve the identified problem; - preserve the announced features; - comply with the applicable license conditions; - not introduce any known security risk. GSA may carry out a new verification before making a corrected version available.

### Article 61 — Refund and amicable resolution

When a major problem cannot reasonably be resolved, GSA may examine with the buyer the possible amicable solutions in accordance with the conditions applicable to purchases. The seller acknowledges that certain situations may lead to a refund request, a cancellation or any other authorized commercial measure. When the problem is directly attributable to the content provided by the seller, the possible financial consequences may be passed on to the amounts due to them under the conditions provided in this contract and within the limits authorized by law.

### Article 62 — Chargeback and payment dispute

When a buyer disputes a transaction with their bank or the payment provider, GSA may request from the seller any information enabling a response to the dispute. The seller notably undertakes to provide, where relevant: - the order information; - the elements demonstrating the provision of the content; - the license information; - the useful exchanges with the buyer; - any other reasonably necessary element. The financial consequences of a dispute may be charged to the seller when they result from a breach directly attributable to them, subject to the payment provider's rules and the applicable regulations.

### Article 63 — Intellectual property claims

When a buyer or a third party reports an alleged intellectual property violation concerning content, GSA may temporarily suspend the content concerned while the situation is examined. GSA may request from the seller: - evidence relating to their rights; - an explanation of the origin of the content; - the licenses concerned; - the removal or replacement of the disputed elements where necessary. The seller remains responsible for holding the rights necessary for the provision of their content.

### Article 64 — No unlimited technical support by GSA

The support provided by GSA is first-level assistance. Unless specifically agreed, it does not include: - the development or complete correction of the seller's content; - the modification of the seller's source code; - the creation of additional features; - compatibility with non-announced environments; - the complete installation of the content on the buyer's infrastructure; - the permanent maintenance of the content. When this type of intervention is necessary, GSA may refer the buyer to the seller or separately offer an adapted service where available.

### Article 65 — Cooperation in case of incident

The seller undertakes to cooperate with GSA for the entire period necessary to handle an incident relating to their content. This obligation notably includes the provision of accurate information and the implementation of corrections reasonably falling within their responsibility. An unjustified refusal or repeated absence of cooperation may result in: - the suspension of the content; - its removal from the platform; - the suspension of the seller account; - the termination of the partnership under the conditions provided in this contract.

### Article 66 — Incident tracking

GSA may retain the information necessary for tracking assistance requests and incidents concerning the seller's content. This information may notably include: - the order reference; - the nature of the problem; - the exchanges with the buyer; - the exchanges with the seller; - the measures taken; - the resolution provided. This information may be retained in accordance with the legal obligations and the applicable data protection policies.

This contract does not entail any transfer of intellectual property from the seller to GSA. Subject to the rights held by third parties, the seller remains the holder of the rights they hold over the content provided to GSA. The provision of content on the GSA platform cannot therefore be interpreted as an assignment of ownership to GSA.

### Article 68 — Rights necessary for publication

The seller warrants holding the rights necessary to allow GSA to use the content within the services provided in this contract. These rights must notably allow, where necessary: - the reception of the content by GSA; - its technical storage; - its verification; - the technical reproduction necessary for the operation of the platform; - its presentation to users; - its promotion; - its provision to the buyer in accordance with the applicable license conditions. The seller remains responsible for verifying that the rights they hold actually cover these uses.

### Article 69 — License granted to GSA

To enable the performance of its services, the seller grants GSA a license to use the content provided. This license is: - non-exclusive; - limited to the needs of GSA services; - non-transferable, except to the technical providers involved in the operation of GSA services where necessary; - granted for the period necessary for the performance of this contract and the obligations arising from it. It notably allows GSA to: - store the content; - verify it; - technically reproduce it where necessary; - present it on the platform; - create presentation materials; - promote it; - provide it to buyers in accordance with the applicable license.

### Article 70 — No assignment of the seller's rights

The license granted to GSA does not constitute an assignment of the seller's intellectual property rights. GSA notably does not become the owner of: - 3D models; - textures; - scripts; - maps; - sounds; - animations; - illustrations; - or any other element belonging to the seller. GSA only benefits from the rights necessary for the performance of its services.

### Article 71 — Elements belonging to third parties

When content includes elements belonging to third parties, the seller warrants holding the authorizations necessary for their use within the content offered. The seller must notably comply with the conditions of the licenses applicable to the elements concerned. When the conditions of a third-party license impose particular restrictions, the seller must communicate them to GSA before the publication of the content. GSA may refuse content when it considers that the rights provided are insufficient or incompatible with its operation.

### Article 72 — Content under a free or open source license

The use of components distributed under a free, open source or similar license remains subject to the specific conditions of that license. The seller must notably comply with the obligations relating to: - redistribution; - attribution; - the retention of license notices; - the conditions of modification; - the distribution of derivative works where applicable. The seller must inform GSA of any particular obligation likely to affect the provision of the content to buyers.

### Article 73 — Seller's warranties concerning rights

The seller declares and warrants, within the limits of their knowledge and the rights they hold, that the provision of their content under the conditions provided in this contract does not deliberately infringe a third party's rights. This warranty notably covers, where applicable: - copyright; - neighbouring rights; - trademarks; - model rights; - rights relating to graphic elements; - rights relating to integrated software or components. The seller undertakes to immediately inform GSA of any claim or procedure they become aware of concerning content offered on the platform.

### Article 74 — Claim by a third party

When a third party claims to hold rights over content offered by the seller, GSA may temporarily suspend the publication of the content concerned to limit risks and allow the examination of the situation. The seller undertakes to provide GSA with the elements reasonably necessary for the analysis of the claim. Depending on the situation, these elements may include: - creation evidence; - licenses; - contracts; - authorizations; - invoices or proof of acquisition; - information relating to the origin of the elements concerned. The temporary suspension of content does not constitute an acknowledgement of the merits of the claim.

### Article 75 — Removal in case of proven violation

When a violation of a third party's rights is established or sufficiently characterized, GSA may remove the content concerned from the platform. The removal may notably occur without notice when maintaining the content presents a significant legal or technical risk. The seller remains responsible for the consequences resulting from the absence of the necessary rights over their content.

### Article 76 — Indemnification of GSA

When the seller's liability is engaged due to a violation of a third party's rights attributable to the content they provided, the seller may be required to bear the consequences directly attributable to this breach, within the limits provided by law. This may notably include, where legally justified: - the costs directly related to the removal of the content; - the amounts due as a result of an established violation; - certain reasonable costs incurred to handle the claim. This provision cannot have the effect of automatically placing on the seller a liability that is not legally attributable to them.

### Article 77 — Creations made by GSA

The original creations made by GSA within its services remain the property of GSA, subject to any third-party rights incorporated. The seller may request the removal of content they offer on GSA. The request must be addressed to GSA in accordance with the arrangements provided for this purpose. GSA may temporarily suspend the seller's access to its services when necessary to protect: - users; - other sellers; - the platform; - transactions; - third-party rights. ### Article 90 — Precautionary measures

When GSA finds a situation likely to cause significant harm, it may take the reasonably necessary measures to limit this risk. These measures may notably include: - the suspension of content; - the temporary suspension of an account; - the cessation of the promotion of content; - the suspension of new orders; - the retention of the elements necessary for the analysis of the situation. The seller may end this contract by sending a termination request to GSA. From the effective date of the termination, GSA may cease: - the publication of the seller's new content; - the promotion of their content; - the acceptance of new orders; - the seller's access to seller-only features.
